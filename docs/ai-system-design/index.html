four levels per module

Every module is written at four depths. Learn the first two everywhere, then go deeper where your target role needs it.

fundamental
Explain what, why and when, and sketch an accurate component diagram. Done when you can draw the diagram and answer three conceptual questions.
intermediate
Design, implement or configure it, compare trade-offs and handle production failures. Done when you can explain a realistic design, two alternatives, failure handling, metrics and how you would build it.
advanced
Optimise hard systems and defend deep design choices at larger scale. Done when your decisions hold as traffic, scale, reliability, security and cost constraints change.
optional
Specialise only when the target role needs research, kernel or infrastructure internals. Done when you can show implementation or research depth.

the interview loop

Seven steps for any staff design question. Run every practice case through them.

  1. Discovery. Confirm workloads, users, constraints, business goal, and out-of-scope items.
  2. Quantification. Estimate QPS, concurrency, tokens/sec, p99, throughput, storage, GPU, and budget.
  3. Architecture. Show major components, request/data flow, boundaries, and storage.
  4. Alternatives. Compare key decisions rather than just naming technologies.
  5. Scaling and failure. Inject traffic spikes, dependency failure, stale ACL, network partition, and capacity limits.
  6. Production readiness. Define monitoring, SLOs, rollback, operational ownership, and emergency controls.
  7. Platform evolution. Explain reusable abstractions, adoption, migration, and cross-team contracts.

coming from data or platform work

  1. Deepen model-serving fundamentals: prefill/decode, batching, KV cache, TTFT/TPOT, GPU memory and scheduling.
  2. Learn AI traffic engineering: admission control, fair queues, throttling, backpressure, and capacity modeling.
  3. Practice tail latency and reliability analysis: SLOs, fallback, bulkheads, load shedding, multi-region.
  4. Strengthen enterprise identity/security design and per-tenant isolation.
  5. Design reusable evaluation, deployment, and monitoring infrastructure.
  6. Practice measurable architectural decisions, including cost models, migrations, operational ownership, and cross-team abstractions.
